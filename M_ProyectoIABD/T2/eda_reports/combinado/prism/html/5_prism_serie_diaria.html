Alerts

True of this data, and worth knowing — but not defects. Nothing here needs fixing; some of it changes how you model.

note

2 level shifts in the series

The most recent is 2011-11-03, where the level stepped up (+42%) and stayed there.

Training across a real level shift teaches a model the average of two regimes that never existed. Consider training only on the most recent one.

note

The level moves over time

Both tests agree the level moves. Difference the series, or use a model that handles a trend directly.

Non-stationarity is a property of the series, not a defect — most series with a trend are non-stationary.

note

The series became harder to predict

Around 2011-08-25 the spread went wider, from 6,929 to 1.09e+04.

Prediction intervals fitted on the whole history will be wrong on both sides of this point.

note

Spread changes too much for a single outlier rule

4% of periods fall outside a fence built from the whole history, which means the spread is not constant rather than that the series is full of anomalies.

Read the variance-shift finding instead; a single outlier threshold is the wrong instrument here.

note

Some weekly seasonality

A weekly cycle explains 49% of what is left after the trend, with a peak-to-trough swing of 7.056e+04.

Use a model with a seasonal term, and never validate on a window shorter than one full cycle.

note

The level is rising

Trend explains 60% of the non-seasonal variation, moving the level by 2,724 across the history.

A model without a trend term will drift steadily wrong as the horizon lengthens.
